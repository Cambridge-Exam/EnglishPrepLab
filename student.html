3. Reading

3 questions · Recommended time: 8–10 minutes

A repair café after school

When Noor suggested a repair café for the school’s community week, she expected a few people to bring broken headphones or torn bags. Instead, the art room filled before lunchtime. Parents arrived with lamps, neighbours brought bicycles and one student carried in a very old radio. The volunteers could not repair everything, but that was not the point. “People stayed to ask questions and watch,” Noor says. “They began to see that a broken object is not automatically rubbish.”

The project also changed the way Noor’s team planned. At first, they had prepared a long list of tools. Later they realised that the most useful thing was a sign explaining each repair. Visitors who had never used a screwdriver felt more comfortable joining in.
